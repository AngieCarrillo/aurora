How I’d engage the whole list

Warm first, so the early yeses become proof I can name in every cold message after.

  1. Week 1, warm

    SoGal, EWA (Rebeca Portilla, who worked with me for Andrés Bilbao), Cibersons, GANAS, WeXchange and Lovable, plus a friend at Replit for extra founder credits. Goal: four or more yeses before anyone cold hears from me.

  2. Weeks 2 to 3, intros

    Amplifica, MAYA, Impacta, Kaszek, Start-Up Chile, Google for Startups, Anthropic, OpenAI for Startups and Nubank, each through the person in my network who knows them.

  3. Weeks 3 to 4, cold

    Cartier. The message opens with a real founder in their thesis and names the partners already in.

Follow-up

Day 0 the message, day 4 a nudge that adds value (two lines on one founder in their thesis), day 10 one clear ask for 20 minutes. No reply means I park them for 60 days and reopen with the next regional shortlist. Once a partner says yes: a monthly digest of three to five founders filtered to their thesis, a quarterly invite to the regional deep-dive, and no more than one direct intro a month, so every intro stays worth opening.

The system

A partner newsletter in ActiveCampaign or Kit, so I can see who opens. Automations push the ones who don’t: a resend with a new subject, then a personal follow-up. In LATAM what works best is WhatsApp, so I message each contact there and confirm their phone and email to keep the list clean. Every partner sits in a personal CRM, the same way I ran Andrés Bilbao’s 100+ company portfolio, tagged by sector, stage, check size, country and role, so matching a founder to investors is a filter.

Full coverage, six months in

  • Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Chile, the Andes and Central America each have two active capital partners and one pipeline partner.
  • Every Aurora sector has at least two investors whose thesis matches.
  • Every regional finalist gets three or more qualified investor meetings within 30 days of the shortlist.
  • A monthly report: active partners, meetings per founder and follow-on capital raised by Aurora alumnae.

Why her

Aurora 2025, 4th place. Arkangel builds AI “digital employees” for hospitals: early disease detection, clinical evidence search, fewer errors. It’s mostly accelerator-funded (Google for Startups Canada, CDL, NEXT Canada), so it’s under-raised for its stage. AI for LATAM hospitals is hot now: a16z led a $33M round in Telepatía in June 2026.

I know Laura personally.

Why US funds

LATAM founders often need to go north for a lead investor. There’s simply more capital there, and a US lead pulls local co-investors in after it.

So I picked funds that just backed Collective X Health, Dr. Stacy Sims' women’s-health data platform ($3.2M pre-seed, Oct 2026). I met the Collective X founders at a pitch last week, and Arkangel is the same bet for hospitals: a B2B intelligence layer built on health data.
